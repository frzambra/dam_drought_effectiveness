Please consider our manuscript, "Dams mark multi-year drought vulnerability rather than buffer it in Chile's megadrought," for publication in Nature Water as an Article.

As warming intensifies drought, governments reflexively build more dams. Socio-hydrology has long warned that this response can be self-defeating, with the reservoir effect and the supply-demand cycle predicting that storage invites demand expansion and erodes adaptation. Yet these feedbacks have been described far more than measured, because reservoirs are built precisely where aridity and irrigation demand already concentrate. Every prior dammed-versus-undammed or before-after comparison inherits this siting confound, so the central question, whether dams build resilience or merely mark preexisting vulnerability, has remained untested for want of a credible counterfactual.

We construct that counterfactual in Chile's megadrought, the longest instrumented drought in the region and an acute testbed where perpetual private water rights forced a purely supply-side policy response. We treat reservoir presence as a basin-level intervention and combine two designs: a matched national comparison of 21 dammed basins against 244 candidate controls sharing climate, size, and elevation, conditioned on the drought each basin actually experienced; and a within-basin placebo comparing regulated reaches below each dam against physically unregulated reaches above it, which share climate, geology, and siting but not the reservoir.

The result is a convergent, bounded null that reverses the standard reading. Dammed basins do transmit meteorological drought into streamflow more gently, the visual signature of buffering, but the attenuation is as strong at unregulated upstream reaches as below the dams, and it collapses once dammed and control basins are compared within the same climate. Across streamflow transmission, cropland change, and consumptive water-rights accrual, the reservoir effect brackets zero under randomization inference. Dammed basins hold about 4.5 times more cropland than matched controls, but the gap is fixed across the 2000 to 2024 record, with flat pre-trends and no megadrought divergence. Meanwhile the storage band itself steps down at the drought onset, peak and trough together, mirroring the independent decline in unregulated control streamflow. These are informative nulls, not absence of power: a positive control recovers injected buffering one-to-one, and the design excludes buffering above 46 to 59 percent of baseline transmission.

We believe this work suits Nature Water for three reasons. First, it gives the contested reservoir-effect hypothesis its first causally identified test, and finds the protective function of storage over-attributed: the buffering dams appear to provide is a property of where they sit, not of what they do. Second, the upstream placebo is a portable method, applicable wherever gauges bracket a dam, that future studies can use to separate regulation from siting. Third, the policy stakes are immediate and general. Drying Mediterranean-climate regions from western North America to Iberia and Australia face the same choice between more storage and demand reform; our results indicate that under structural aridification, building more storage is a supply-blind response to a supply problem, and adaptation must shift toward demand management and allocation.
