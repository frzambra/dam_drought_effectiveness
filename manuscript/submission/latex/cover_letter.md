Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, "An upstream placebo shows that reservoir siting explains the
apparent drought buffering of seasonal reservoirs in Chile's megadrought," for publication in
Water Resources Research as a Research Article.

Dams are built where water is already scarce, so every dammed-versus-undammed or before-after
comparison inherits a siting confound, and national-scale studies of regulation effects say as
much themselves: regulated and near-natural catchments are not distributed homogeneously across
hydroclimatic zones. Model-based counterfactuals avoid that confound but substitute another,
since conceptual storage models cannot accumulate deficits across years and are therefore
weakest at exactly the multi-year timescale at which drought buffering matters. The result is
that the protective function attributed to storage has been described far more often than it has
been identified.

We construct the counterfactual inside the basin. If a dam buffers drought, the transmission of
meteorological deficit into streamflow deficit must flatten below the dam and not at the
unregulated reaches above it, which share climate, geology, and the entire history that sited
the dam. This upstream/downstream placebo is the paper's central identification strategy, and we
pair it with a matched comparison of 21 dammed basins against 244 candidate controls sharing
climate, size, and elevation, evaluating both against the drought each basin actually
experienced rather than against calendar time.

Across Chile's megadrought the attenuation is as strong upstream as downstream (slopes -0.20
versus -0.16; paired contrast p = 0.72) and collapses once basins are compared within their own
climate region, so for the seasonal majority of the fleet the apparent buffering is a property
of where dams sit. The null is powered rather than blind: a positive control recovers injected
buffering one to one, and the design excludes operational buffering above 46 to 59% of baseline
transmission. The exception is physical and, we are explicit, exploratory: the seven
carryover-capable reservoirs, those holding at least half a year of downstream flow, do show a
downstream-specific contrast (-0.23, p = 0.014) whose direction survives every leave-one-out and
the capacity classification, though its significance survives neither. Dammed basins hold about
4.5 times more cropland than matched controls, but that gap is a stable siting legacy with no
megadrought divergence detected within the design's power.

We believe the work fits Water Resources Research on three grounds. It is a methodological
contribution first: the upstream placebo is portable to any regulated river with gauges above
and below a dam, its scope conditions are stated explicitly, and every null is reported with the
effect size it can exclude rather than as an absence of evidence. It is a substantive
contribution to drought propagation, separating operation from siting in the transmission of
multi-year meteorological deficit, and quantifying a storage band that steps down at the drought
onset while the carryover floor keeps eroding. And it carries a direct water-resources
implication: under structural aridification, expanding seasonal storage is a supply-blind
response to a supply problem, and the capacity threshold we identify points to where storage
still plausibly helps.

The manuscript reports original work, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been
published previously. I am the sole author and declare no competing interests. The complete
analysis pipeline, the reservoir dataset, and all result tables and figures are openly archived
on Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21052149) and on GitHub, and are cited in the Open
