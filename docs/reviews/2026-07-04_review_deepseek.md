# Assessment of Fit for Nature Water

## Overall Verdict: Potentially suitable with substantial revisions

This manuscript presents a rigorous causal inference study examining whether reservoirs buffer or merely reflect drought vulnerability during Chile's megadrought. The work is methodologically sophisticated, addresses a timely and globally relevant question, and offers counterintuitive findings that challenge conventional wisdom about dam construction as drought adaptation.

However, in its current form, it does not meet Nature Water's standards for presentation, accessibility, and framing. Below I provide a detailed assessment and specific recommendations.
Major Issues Preventing Acceptance

1. Scope and Framing Mismatch

Problem: The manuscript is written as a specialist hydrology methods paper rather than a broad, impactful study for an interdisciplinary water journal. Nature Water publishes work that bridges natural sciences, engineering, social sciences, and policy.

Recommendations:

- Reframe the narrative around the central policy dilemma: "Should governments continue building dams as drought adaptation?" rather than "Does the reservoir effect exist?"

- Lead with the policy relevance in the first paragraph: Chile's 1981 Water Code, the global pattern of diminishing dam returns, and the urgency of adaptation under climate change

- Add a dedicated "Policy Implications" section (not just buried in Discussion) that explicitly addresses:

- What should Chile's water policy reform prioritize instead of dams?

- How do these findings inform the global dam-building debate?

- What are the governance conditions under which dams might work differently?

2. Insufficient Contextualization for Broader Audience

Problem: The manuscript assumes extensive knowledge of Chilean water governance, drought indices, and causal inference methods. Nature Water readers include policymakers, economists, ecologists, and engineers who may not be specialists in all these areas.

Recommendations:

- Expand the introduction to explain:

- Why Chile is a critical case study (not just "acute testbed" but a clear description of the water rights system, agricultural economy, and climate vulnerability)

- What the "reservoir effect" and "supply-demand cycle" mean in plain language with concrete examples

- The global significance of these findings (e.g., how many other regions face similar dynamics?)

- Add a glossary or explanatory box for key terms: SPEI, SSI, entropy balancing, randomization inference, ATT

- Simplify methods language in the main text (e.g., "matched comparison" rather than "doubly-robust matched ATT estimator")

3. Overreliance on Supplementary Information

Problem: Critical methodological details, robustness checks, and even some results are relegated to supplementary materials. This makes it difficult for readers to assess the validity of findings without extensive digging.

Recommendations:

- Move key methods from SI to main text:

- The matching procedure (entropy balancing) deserves a clear main-text description

- The within-basin placebo design is central to the argument and should be fully explained in main text

- The positive control (injected buffering recovery) should be in main text to demonstrate power

- Reduce SI dependency by integrating at least 2-3 key SI tables/figures into the main text

4. Statistical Jargon and Presentation Barriers

Problem: The manuscript uses dense statistical language that will alienate non-specialist readers. Examples:

- "doubly-robust matched ATT estimator"

- "forcing-interacted DiD slope gaps"

- "randomization-inference p-values"

- "entropy balancing exact-matched within Köppen main group"

Recommendations:

- Define all statistical terms in plain English the first time they appear

- Use concrete examples to illustrate statistical concepts (e.g., "Think of this like comparing two groups of patients with the same disease severity...")

- Create a "Methods in Brief" summary after the results (2-3 paragraphs explaining the logic of the design without equations)

- Move technical statistical details to an expanded Methods section, but keep the main text accessible

5. Figures Need Better Design for Impact

Problem: The figures are clear to specialists but could be more compelling and accessible for a broader audience.

Recommendations:

- Figure 2: Add a simple schematic explaining what each panel shows (not just "buffering is siting")

- Figure 3: The standardized-effects plot is excellent but needs:

- Clearer labeling of what constitutes a meaningful effect

- A note about why water-rights appears to show an effect but doesn't survive permutation tests

- Figure 4: Add a panel showing the actual cropland difference (11.1% vs 2.4%) more prominently

- Figure 5: Add a conceptual diagram showing what the amplitude trend means for reservoir function

Consider a graphical abstract showing the central finding: dams sit where water is scarce vs. dams create resilience

Additional Specific Issues

6. Water Rights Analysis Needs Clarification

The water-rights findings are confusing: the count shows a naive effect that doesn't survive permutation tests, while the winsorized volume has a p=0.077 under spatial inference.

Recommendation: This complexity needs more careful explanation. Readers will struggle to understand why the "apparent induced demand" isn't robust. The current explanation ("dammed basins are more arid") could be illustrated with a simple figure showing aridity gradients.

7. Missing Discussion of Alternative Explanations

While the manuscript tests many confounders, the discussion of limitations feels rushed and technical.

Recommendations:

- Add a dedicated "Limitations" subsection in the Discussion (not just scattered caveats)

- Explicitly acknowledge what cannot be concluded:

        The study doesn't test sub-seasonal buffering

        It can't assess construction-phase induced demand

        Groundwater substitution remains uncertain

- Address the "so what" question more directly: If dams don't buffer drought, what should policymakers do?

8. Language and Accessibility

Problem: Many sentences are grammatically complex and difficult to follow.

Examples:

    "Because treatment is time-invariant, we test the spatial imprint of these feedbacks, not the longitudinal loop: the 4.6-fold cropland gap could be the equilibrated legacy of demand induced at construction, so the null scopes to the megadrought era, not historical induced demand, testable only with staggered commissioning"

Recommendation: Break long sentences into shorter, clearer statements. Use active voice. Write for an interdisciplinary audience.
Strengths to Emphasize

The manuscript has several strengths that align well with Nature Water:

-  Novel causal identification strategy - The within-basin placebo is elegant and addresses a long-standing problem in water resources research

-  Counterintuitive finding - The conclusion that dams mark vulnerability rather than buffer it challenges dominant policy narratives

-  Multi-evidence approach - The study examines multiple pathways (streamflow, cropland, water rights, storage), increasing confidence in findings

-  Policy relevance - The findings have direct implications for Chile's water reform and global dam-building debates

-  Rigorous sensitivity analysis - The positive control, hard-balanced matching, and spatial inference address common criticisms

# Conclusion

This is important and rigorous science that deserves publication in a top-tier journal. However, Nature Water requires not just technical excellence but also policy relevance, accessibility, and broad appeal. The current manuscript is written for a specialist hydrology audience and would be rejected for lack of accessibility and framing mismatch.

With substantial revision focusing on clarity, policy implications, and interdisciplinary accessibility, this manuscript could be a strong fit for Nature Water. The scientific contribution is there; the presentation needs to match the journal's scope.
Recommended Next Steps

- Reframe the paper around the policy question: "Does building dams during drought create resilience or just reflect existing vulnerability?"

- Simplify the statistical presentation in the main text

- Expand the policy implications and limitations

- Improve figure design and accessibility

- Consider having a non-specialist read the introduction and results sections to identify jargon and unclear passages

- Shorten the methods-heavy sections and move technical details to SI

- Add a clear statement of what policymakers should do differently based on these findings

The work is significant enough that these revisions would be worthwhile. Good luck!
