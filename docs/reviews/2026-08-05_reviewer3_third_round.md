# Fatal Flaws

1. Placebo test confounded by unadjusted elevation gradient

The central conclusion that reservoirs show no measurable buffer against multi-year drought relies heavily on the upstream/downstream placebo test, which finds statistically identical transmission slopes upstream and downstream (-0.20 vs -0.16). However, the authors also demonstrate that transmission slopes are highly sensitive to elevation among control gauges (-0.21 per km) and that upstream gauges sit on average 0.54 km higher than downstream gauges. This elevation difference introduces a systematic confound: higher elevation is associated with steeper (more negative) transmission slopes, which means the upstream gauges would naturally be expected to have steeper slopes than downstream gauges by about 0.11 units. Failing to formally adjust the primary placebo contrast for this elevation difference leaves the comparison confounded, as the apparent similarity in raw slopes may mask a true downstream buffering effect that is offset by the elevation difference. The authors should perform an elevation-adjusted placebo test to confirm whether the null result holds after controlling for this geomorphic gradient.
