# Fatal Flaws

1. Truncated streamflow record

The streamflow daily record used to construct SSI-12 ends in March 2020, thereby completely omitting the most acute phase of Chile's megadrought (the peak deficits of 2019–2022). Because operational buffering is most likely to be deployed and detectable during the most extreme multi-year deficits, evaluating streamflow only up to 2020 severely limits the study's ability to claim that no operational buffering occurred during the megadrought. The authors should extend the streamflow record to cover the full megadrought period up to 2024 to capture the peak deficit years.

2. Downstream gauge misclassification risk

The classification of gauges as "upstream" or "downstream" of dams is based solely on SRTM elevation relative to the reservoir outlet, without validation against routed network topology. In steep, highly branched Andean catchments, a gauge at a lower elevation than a dam can easily be located on an unregulated parallel tributary rather than the regulated main stem. If "downstream" gauges are actually unregulated, the downstream transmission slope would naturally match the upstream slope, artificially producing a null difference and biasing the placebo test toward finding "no operational buffering." This is a critical threat to the paper's central identification strategy. The authors should validate the gauge locations against routed network topology to ensure downstream gauges are truly on the regulated main stems.

3. Inability to rule out moderate buffering

The study's streamflow analysis is only powered to exclude operational buffering above 46% to 59% of baseline. This means that moderate but highly consequential buffering effects (e.g., a 30% or 40% reduction in drought transmission) cannot be ruled out and are fully compatible with the data. Presenting this as a definitive "no detected operational buffering" in the headline is an over-generalization of a wide null. The authors should temper the headline and abstract to clarify that only very large buffering effects can be ruled out, while moderate buffering remains possible.

4. Water-rights registry blind to market reallocations

The water-rights analysis relies on the DGA registry of new state-granted rights to measure demand expansion. However, because central Chilean basins have been administratively closed to new allocations for decades, the accrual of new grants is legally blocked and mechanically near zero. Actual demand expansion and concentration during the megadrought occur through private market reallocations (transactions registered in local real-estate offices) and groundwater pumping, which the DGA registry does not record. Thus, the null result for water-rights accrual is an artifact of institutional rules and registry limitations, not evidence of a lack of demand expansion. The authors should temper the headline claim regarding demand expansion or acknowledge that the registry is blind to market-driven reallocation.

5. Severe underpowering of cropland expansion

The cropland expansion analysis has a Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) of 7,330% of baseline. With such extremely low statistical power, the failure to detect a post-2010 divergence in cropland area is a statistical certainty rather than evidence of "no demand expansion." Promoting this null finding to a headline conclusion is highly misleading. The authors should explicitly qualify the headline claim to reflect that the cropland analysis is severely underpowered to detect realistic expansion rates.
