# Fatal Flaws

1. Streamflow record excludes peak megadrought years

The headline claim asserts that reservoir siting, rather than regulation, explains the apparent drought buffering observed during Chile's megadrought. However, the primary hydrological evidence (the streamflow transmission analysis) relies on a record that ends in March 2020, thereby excluding the most acute and severe phase of the megadrought (2019–2022). While storage and cropland data extend to 2024, streamflow is the only direct measure of hydrological drought transmission. Excluding the peak deficit years limits the ability to generalize the 'no operational buffering' conclusion to the entire megadrought, as reservoir operations and buffering capacity may behave differently under extreme, multi-year stress. The authors should qualify the headline claim to clarify that the direct hydrological null is restricted to the first decade of the megadrought and does not cover the peak deficit years.

# Reviewer Comments

1. Causal attribution of storage decline is unsupported

The claim asserts that the downward drift in reservoir storage is 'due to declining supply rather than operational failure.' However, the authors acknowledge in the text that this trend analysis is purely descriptive, lacks a control group, and is confounded with potential management changes, meaning it 'supports no causal attribution.' Attributing the decline definitively to supply-side factors rather than operational or management shifts oversteps what the descriptive data can show. The authors should temper this causal claim to reflect the descriptive and confounded nature of the storage trend analysis.

2. Catchment area confound in placebo test

The within-basin placebo test compares upstream (unregulated) and downstream (regulated) gauges. However, upstream gauges naturally have smaller drainage areas than downstream gauges. In hydrology, larger catchment areas typically provide greater natural storage and longer routing times, which can independently attenuate (flatten) drought transmission. The authors do not control for or quantify the effect of drainage area on the transmission slope among control gauges, as they did for elevation. This systematic difference in catchment scale between upstream and downstream gauges could confound the placebo comparison, potentially masking or distorting any reservoir-specific operational effect. The authors should conduct a sensitivity analysis of the transmission slope to catchment area among undammed control gauges to rule out this confound.

3. Water-rights registry misses private transactions

The operationalization of water-rights accrual using the DGA registry is problematic because it only records original state grants and not private market transactions. Since many Chilean basins have been administratively closed to new allocations for decades, the lack of new state grants is a regulatory artifact rather than evidence of no demand expansion. Furthermore, private transactions (which are registered in local real-estate offices) are completely invisible in this dataset, meaning any market-driven concentration of water rights below reservoirs cannot be detected. The authors should explicitly limit the scope of this claim or temper the conclusion that reservoirs do not induce water-rights concentration.

4. Cropland null is a bounded null

The claim states that the cropland gap is a stable siting legacy with no post-2010 megadrought divergence. However, the authors acknowledge in the text that the post-2015 permutation envelope widens significantly, meaning the analysis cannot rule out substantial divergence in the later years of the megadrought. Thus, the 'no divergence' finding is a bounded null due to a lack of statistical power rather than a confirmed absence of divergence. The authors should qualify the claim to reflect this limitation, rather than presenting it as a definitive null.
