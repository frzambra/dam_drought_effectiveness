Project: Reservoir Effectiveness and Drought Vulnerability in Chile. Target: Nature Water.

**Core question:** Do reservoirs reduce drought impacts, or primarily DELAY them while increasing long-term vulnerability via induced demand (expansion of water-dependent land uses)?

**Pathway:** Meteorological drought -> hydrological drought -> reservoir storage -> water availability -> agricultural expansion -> ecological response -> long-term vulnerability.

**Hypotheses (in docs/hypotheses/, internally adversarially reviewed):**
- H2 (score 9.4, central narrative): induced demand erases the buffer; net protection decays toward zero within ~a decade. Load-bearing test = MEDIATION (does cumulative irrigated-area/ET expansion mediate the buffer decline?).
- H1 (8.8, mechanism / most transformative): storage acts as a nonlinear THRESHOLD RECTIFIER — attenuates small droughts but redistributes variance into the tail past a storage threshold s*. Key test = counterfactual-overshoot below s*.
- H4 (8.3): ecological lock-in (resistance vs resilience loss).
- H3 (8.1, riskiest): spatial displacement of vulnerability.

**Single message:** Reservoirs in Chile postpone and concentrate drought vulnerability rather than reducing it.

**Cross-cutting confounds (must design against):** endogenous reservoir siting; 2010-present mega-drought collinear with treatment clock; drought-vs-land-conversion attribution in NDVI/ET; n~=26 reservoirs caps generalizability (frame as "Chile as natural laboratory").
