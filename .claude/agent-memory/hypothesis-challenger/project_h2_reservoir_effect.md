H2 ("Reservoir Effect / Maladaptive Equilibrium"): a reservoir's drought-buffering capacity is endogenously consumed by the agricultural expansion it enables, so NET drought protection of a basin declines toward zero within ~a decade. Mechanism: storage lowers perceived irrigation risk -> irrigated area & consumptive ET expand -> buffer committed -> no slack in next drought -> higher-demand, lower-resilience equilibrium.

**Dominant confound (load-bearing):** Chile's 2010-present megadrought is collinear with time-since-reservoir. A secular decline in net protection over the study window can be produced by progressive aquifer depletion / declining inflows alone, with ZERO behavioral induced demand. Any "buffer erodes over time" finding must rule this out.

**Other key threats:** endogenous reservoir placement & timing (built where expansion was already planned -> reverse causation); NDVI/ET attribution (drought stress vs. land conversion produce opposite-signed NDVI moves — must decompose); SUTVA (upstream-downstream hydrologic connectivity, water markets reallocating across basins); storage data is a relative index not volume.

**Most decisive falsification test:** mediation must show the buffer-erosion effect flows THROUGH cumulative irrigated-area/ET expansion. If drought-sensitivity worsens over time in dammed basins WITHOUT a matching irrigated-area increase (direct effect dominates, indirect via area ~0), the induced-demand mechanism is falsified even if the buffer appears to erode. Available EO data (MODIS, irrigated-area maps) CAN produce this test once acquired.

**Verdict held:** the descriptive pattern is likely real and publishable; the specific induced-demand MECHANISM is the fragile, novel claim and is currently unidentified against the megadrought confound + endogenous placement. See [[data-state]].
