Recurring alternative explanations to rule out before crediting a reservoir "rectifier" / amplification claim:

1. **Demand-side ratchet (induced expansion):** reservoirs enable irrigated-area expansion in wet/normal years; when storage finally fails, a larger water-dependent footprint collapses -> heavier vegetation/ET drought tail. This is the project's OWN main hypothesis (CLAUDE.md) and is observationally near-identical to "rectification." Discriminate with land-cover/irrigated-area trajectories + ET trends.
2. **Selection/placement bias:** dams sited in already drought-prone, high-demand, high-variability basins. Heavier tails are pre-existing, not caused. Check pre-2010 / pre-treatment baseline tails and matching balance.
3. **Operating policy (not physics):** detected threshold = rule curve / dead storage. See [[endogenous-operation-rules]].
4. **Regression to the mean / mechanical floor:** storage bounded at 0; once empty, downstream tracks meteorology with no extra signal — apparent "amplification" is just removal of buffering, not active amplification.
5. **EO/measurement artifacts:** reservoir surface area shrinking exposes bare soil/pixels -> NDVI/EVI drop attributed to "ecological drought"; mixed pixels near the reservoir. Mask water/shoreline pixels.
6. **Mega-drought shared shock:** see [[megadrought-shared-shock]].

**How to apply:** For any amplification/heavier-tail finding, walk this list and require a discriminating test per item before mechanistic interpretation. Note (1) and the rectifier hypothesis may be statistically indistinguishable with available data — flag as identification problem, not just robustness.
