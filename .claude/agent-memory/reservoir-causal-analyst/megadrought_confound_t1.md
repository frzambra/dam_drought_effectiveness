T1 (flagged by hypothesis-challenger, applies to BOTH H1 and H2): the 2010-present Chilean mega-drought is collinear with calendar time and roughly with reservoir-era exposure. A monotone decline in basin drought protection is EXACTLY what hydrological exhaustion produces with ZERO induced demand. Calendar time alone cannot separate the behavioral mechanism (H2) from exhaustion.

**Why it is make-or-break:** any design whose treatment intensity is just "years since 2005/2010" is observationally identical to "cumulative precipitation deficit." Reviewer #2 sinks the paper on this alone.

**How to break it (use several; triangulate):**
1. Condition out the meteorological forcing directly: the outcome is the basin RESPONSE FUNCTION (downstream availability / ET / NDVI) conditional on basin-specific SPI/SPEI/EDDI at matched accumulation timescales. Estimate effect on the deficit->impact slope, not on the level.
2. Dose-response across reservoirs experiencing the SAME drought year: under pure exhaustion, protection decay should not depend on storage slack or capacity-per-command-area; under induced demand (H2) it should. Cross-sectional dose variation within a drought year is forcing-orthogonal.
3. Dammed vs matched-undammed controls EXPERIENCING THE SAME mega-drought: differences out the common meteorological shock (DiD-in-spirit). Requires controls matched on PRE-TRENDS (drought-response slope pre-2010), not just levels.
4. The ~5 true in-panel commissioning switches (see [[treatment-design-constraints]]) as a placebo/corroboration: but note Convento_Viejo/Aromos commission AT mega-drought onset — anticipation + confound; treat skeptically.
5. Placebo/pre-period: drought-response slope should NOT differ dammed-vs-control in 1991-2009 (needs index back to 1991 — available) if matching is valid.

**Falsifier for H2 specifically:** if the storage-availability erosion is fully explained by cumulative SPEI deficit and shows NO additional erosion mediated by irrigated-area/ET expansion (mediated effect ~ 0 once forcing conditioned), the induced-demand mechanism is rejected.
